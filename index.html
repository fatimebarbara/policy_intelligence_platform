01 / Approach

From reactive policy to a cyclical, evidence-fed process.

Policy problems are compound. Their solutions have to be adjusted to the territory they are meant for — not borrowed from elsewhere and hoped into place.

The increasing complexity of the policymaking context calls for compound policies built on extended analytical capacity: large datasets synthesised through data-science techniques, translated into scenarios a decision-maker can actually use. The platform provides such scenarios for policy priority setting, implementation and monitoring, generating foresight for better-adjusted decisions.

The aim is to help policymaking become proactive rather than reactive: identifying the economic, social or environmental opportunities and needs of a given territory, and then measuring whether the decisions taken in response are having the intended effect. Evidence feeds the next cycle. Awareness becomes agency.

Every method here grew out of more than a decade of work on research and innovation strategies, innovation ecosystems and city intelligence — inside the European Commission's Joint Research Centre, and since then in independent research on how cities and regions build the structural capacity to learn, adapt and improve.

03 / Knowledge assets

Three methodologies, each answering a question a policymaker actually asks.

Identifying high-potential sustainable market dynamics

Q · Where should this territory place its bets — and what is the greenest bet within reach?

Based on the synthesis of large trade and capability datasets, the method identifies groups of high- and low-potential products linked to policy priority areas and proposes forecasts for those domains. It reveals the intangible capabilities embedded in what a territory already produces and trades, and uses their interconnections to map evidence-based pathways for structural change towards higher value-added activity.

To support the Sustainable Development Goals, the method also identifies the closest green alternatives — products a territory could plausibly move into, given what it already knows how to make. The result feeds strategic decisions and policy design, and creates a cyclical learning process between expected and actual results.

Monitoring the impact of policy decisions

Q · Did the instrument we funded change anything — and should we keep funding it?

The method monitors the change of a given sector, sub-sector or group of products supported by policy instruments, so that the impact and effectiveness of the decision can be assessed. By tracking the evolution of specific markets or products at regular intervals, governments see the challenges and opportunities emerging from future industry trajectories before they arrive.

Feeding this back into the territory's own policy process lets decision-makers confirm the validity and relevance of previously selected priorities — or revise them — and respond to future challenges proactively rather than after the fact. It builds on a decade of work designing monitoring and evaluation systems for research and innovation strategies across Europe.

Uncovering geographies of innovation

Q · Where is innovation actually concentrating in our city — and which concentrations will succeed?

Innovation districts are emerging worldwide, and they need targeted, integrative policy to turn their positive externalities into structural change for the whole place. The method identifies urban areas with a high concentration of innovation activity — including activity linked to sustainability — from large-scale geospatial data, and measures the differentiators between areas that flourish and areas that stall.

The aim is to inform policy engineering: which districts to support, with which instruments, and how to judge whether the support is working. The methodology was developed with network scientists and published by the European Commission's Joint Research Centre, alongside case studies from Amsterdam, Barcelona, Melbourne and Stockholm.

04 / Evidence base

The platform is not a product built from scratch. Each capability traces to a specific body of research, most of it produced inside the European Commission's Joint Research Centre and continued independently since.

2014 – 2017

Research and innovation strategy foundations

Regional benchmarking, the role of universities, innovation in rural areas, peer-review workshops, the Stairway to Excellence country work and the thematic platform on industrial modernisation.

2018 – 2021

Monitoring, evaluation & interregional learning

Evaluation frameworks for thematic interregional partnerships, monitoring and evaluation experience across Europe, leadership in interregional collaboration, and the policy experience of research and innovation strategies seen from national and regional authorities.

2021

Geographies of innovation

A quantitative methodology to identify urban innovation districts and measure their success, plus thirteen case studies across four cities on how park, hub, district and ecosystem models differ.

ongoing

Capabilities & structural change

Economic-complexity methods that read the intangible capabilities embedded in what a territory produces and trades, and identify its highest- and lowest-potential product categories — evidence-based pathways towards higher value-added activity.

2025 →

City intelligence

The Future of Cities (2025) and the City Intelligence Index: a six-pillar comparative framework of urban structural performance across European NUTS2 city-regions, currently in data population and validation.

Researcher, keynote speaker and educator working at the intersection of urban strategy, city intelligence and AI-enabled governance. For more than a decade at the European Commission's Joint Research Centre she worked on the EU's place-based research and innovation strategies, including their monitoring and evaluation, interregional collaboration and the geographies of innovation inside cities.

Her book The Future of Cities (2025) argues that cities are living systems, shaped by what their leaders choose to measure. That argument now takes applied form in the City Intelligence Index and the Future of Cities Learning Hub, where policymakers, researchers and city leaders learn to diagnose a city's structural capacity and act on it.

She holds a PhD on market-oriented city development and is also a published literary author and a painter working in watercolour — which is to say that she reads a city with both a spreadsheet and a sketchbook, and is convinced the two belong together.
